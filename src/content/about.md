Surgery has been my goal since my pre-university years at Deeksha, when the rest of the class moved between the specimens and I stayed glued to the brain model, trying to work out which part did what.

My neurosurgery year was the hardest I’ve worked yet. I was the first-line resident for the surgical unit, with the overnight on-call duties and responsibilities, and I ran EduEcho, the peer-teaching platform I’d founded, alongside it. Those nights taught me to think quickly at the bedside: assess, decide, act, and make the right call to the consultant early.

I like a problem that has a fix. At Akshaya I built the operating list most days around surgeons, staff and equipment, and adapted the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist until it reached full compliance at re-audit. During COVID I helped coordinate home-isolation monitoring for more than 200,000 people in Bengaluru.

I’d rather meet a long shift or a hard task with a smile, and captaining football teams from school to medical school showed me how much a team takes its mood from the person in front. I haven’t stopped learning either: ATLS, CCrISP and a neuroradiology certificate since 2025, 9,097 papers analysed in R for my first first-author paper, Mandarin for fun, and now an MRes in Clinical Sciences here in Liverpool, a city I chose after watching how the doctors at the Walton Centre asked their questions.

What I’ve learnt so far is that technical excellence in isolation isn’t care, and I’m working towards the surgeon I strive to be, one whose work reaches further than his own operating list.
